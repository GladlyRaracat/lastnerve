The Opossum Plan · free from Last Nerve

Leaving the house without the flop.

Why it keeps happening. Going limp, stalling, and losing a shoe all delay something your kid would rather skip. If the flop buys even five extra minutes, it worked, and behavior that works comes back. The plan below makes moving pay off faster than stalling does.

The plays

  1. Give two heads-ups. One at 10 minutes, one at 2. Use the same words every time: "Ten minutes, then shoes."
  2. Shrink the first step. "Shoes on" is one job. "Get ready" is six jobs wearing a trench coat.
  3. Say it once, as a statement. "Time for shoes." Drop the "okay?" at the end, because that turns it into a question.
  4. Catch the first move. The second a hand touches a shoe, say what you saw: "You grabbed your shoe. Nice."
  5. Put the good stuff after the door. The song, the snack, the window seat. First car, then the thing.
  6. If the flop happens, get boring. Stay close and calm. Wait about a minute, repeat the same short instruction once, and help with the smallest piece.

What to stop

  • Repeating the instruction six times
  • Bargaining or adding new offers mid-flop
  • Letting a flop earn extra time on the tablet
  • The lecture in the driveway

What to expect

  • Days 1 to 3 can get louder before they get better. That is normal when stalling stops working.
  • Look for the trend across the week, since single mornings bounce around.
